## What this app has to do

Help the band design **unconventional but strong song forms**, carried by our own textures and material, at the level of construction we hear in Tool. Success means:

1. Every EP track is a deliberate formal experiment we can explain: what returns, where the climax is, what carries the energy.
2. The drummer and Ali can **design a form before the material exists** and hand it to the band as a plan.
3. By the main project, we have our own formal principles, written down, that don't come from any one band.

## What the research changed

The search found that no book explains Tool's architecture. Only three academic texts analyze Tool at all, and none covers *Schism*, *The Grudge* or *The Patient*. What the literature does theorize well are the levers that show up in Tool's music. With the band's own account of how it writes, that gives **one process and three structural levers**:

- **Process.** Jam, record everything, name and count each riff on a riff board, assemble the form, and write vocals last, over a settled structure.
- **Lever 1: frame vs. cycle.** Riffs of odd lengths rotate inside a steady metric frame. *The frame is the form, and the misalignment is the content.*
- **Lever 2: climax placement.** The climax arrives late, once, built up by texture rather than harmony: terminal and cumulative forms.
- **Lever 3: metric dissonance as a section marker.** Tool's choruses are often *less* metrically stable than its verses, the reverse of the rock norm.

Underneath everything is **memory**. Forms that don't return lose the listener unless motive, groove or timbre gives them something to hold. That's why Module 1 (material and identity) stays first.

<KeyIdea>No book explains Tool. Their process plus three well-theorized levers does. Our own transcriptions fill the rest.</KeyIdea>

## The listener layer (v3)

Form happens twice: once on the page, and once in the listener's memory, expectation and body. Every structural decision is also a decision about attention, tension and felt time. So psychology isn't a separate subject. It runs through every module in two ways:

1. **Every concept page gets a "Why it works on the listener" section.** It's graded by evidence: strong, moderate, weak or speculative.
2. **Module 5, *The listener*, gathers it.** It covers attention and repetition, tension curves, groove and entrainment, chills and anticipation, trance and absorption, persona and catharsis. Its tool is a **listening-test kit**: demos played to 3–5 people who don't know the material, asking where attention drifted, where they started counting, and where they dropped in.

Five findings change how we design:

- **Listeners barely hear large-scale proportions and key plans** (Cook; Lalitte & Bigand). Fibonacci and golden sections are scaffolding for us. Audible landmarks (motif, timbre, density) have to carry the form.
- **Tension follows loudness, register, density and tempo more than harmony** (Farbood). Builds are written as curves of those four.
- **Odd meters are learned through exposure** (Hannon & Trehub). State them plainly, repeat them, then displace them. Keep one anchor layer (Vuust & Witek).
- **Anticipation is rewarding in itself** (Salimpoor). Give each climax 15 seconds or more of audible approach.
- **Trance is invited, not caused** (Rouget, Fachner). A long ostinato fades into the background (Hove), and slow audible change on top holds the mind (Reich). Alternate driving, overloaded sections with still, sparse ones (Rouget's trance vs. ecstasy).

Hypnosis ideas from Erickson and Elman (pacing and leading, fractionation, confusion followed by resolution) are kept as **craft analogies, clearly labeled as such, and tested** with the listening kit. They are never presented as evidence.

The biggest open gap: **no study uses prog or metal, odd-meter groove, or music longer than about 6 minutes.** Our listening tests and the Tool lab are how we close it for ourselves.

## How this blueprint changes

- **When a source is read in full:** update *Coverage*, revise the concept pages it feeds, log the change.
- **When the Tool lab finishes a song map:** add it as evidence to the relevant concepts. That's how the unfilled gaps get filled.
- **When the EP teaches us something:** it goes in the journal first, then into "our form principles" here.
