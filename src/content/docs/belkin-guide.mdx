## Alan Belkin, *Musical Composition: Craft and Art* — chapter guide

Read in full, November 2026 (ch. 9, 14 and 19 skimmed: they apply the principles to binary, rondo and sonata practice forms). This guide is a paraphrase for finding your way back into the book, not a substitute for it. Each chapter lists its main ideas, what they mean for a band writing long riff-based songs, and where the app uses them.

Belkin's own framing: the chapter titles are **verbs** because they're things the music has to do. The textbook forms are practice; the principles are style-neutral.

## Part 1 — Materials

### Ch. 1 · Motive
- A motive is the smallest memorable unit, and **internal contrast** is what makes it memorable. Contrast can be in pitch, rhythm, or more rarely timbre, articulation, register, dynamics.
- **Similarity scale 1–5** for variants: transposition and enlarged leaps (1); inversion with rhythm kept (2); new contour (3); steps turned to leaps, augmentation, diminution (4); retrograde, added repeated notes, swapped accent pattern (5). Articulation is part of rhythm. Context changes the rating.
- Close variants within a section, medium ones in transitions, distant ones as contrasting material far away. Save vivid contrasts.
- Double motives (a/b reordered), neutral lines that let music breathe, motivic wandering with a clear return, ornamental variants held for a whole section.
- **App:** Module 1 — *What makes a motive*, *Degrees of similarity*, *Developing variation*, *The sentence and liquidation*.

### Ch. 2 · Phrase
- A phrase is a clause, sized by attention and short-term memory. Structure in miniature: a beginning (≤3 motives), an incremental progression to one local peak, punctuation whose strength depends on position.
- Opening phrases stable; fragmentary structures later. Fragments building to a stable phrase make the arrival feel like resolution.
- **App:** *Phrase, grouping and memory*.

### Ch. 3 · Singing
- Voice carries meaning beyond words (register, tone, speed). Middle range comfortable, extremes cost more and mean more.
- Text first: mark stresses, speak to find the natural contour; the music may exaggerate it but never contradict it. Open vowels on climactic notes.
- Recitative (information) vs. aria (emotion) as two modes. Singing takes two to three times as long as speaking. Musical punctuation follows the text's levels.
- **App:** *The vocal line*; Module 6 (planned).

### Ch. 4 · Playing (ensemble writing)
- Ensemble as a democracy: parts agree, disagree, comment, accompany, stop. Conserve resources: rest an instrument or a register before its big moment.
- **Primary dimensions**: tempo, timbre, register, dynamics. Plan them when laying out form; harmony alone won't make a dramatic contrast.
- Voice and band: for equal intensity, alternate; doubling the voice is a climax device.
- **App:** *Building tension* (holding back); *Contrast and transition*.

### Ch. 5 · Punctuating
- Punctuation as hierarchical segmentation. A cadence needs at least two dimensions closing, usually with rhythmic relaxation. Melodic, harmonic, rhythmic, dynamic and textural closure.
- **Degrees of punctuation 1–10**. "Yes, but…" punctuation keeps momentum: let one dimension contradict the others. Elision. Silence works only after something incomplete.
- **App:** Module 4 — *Degrees of punctuation*.

## Part 2 — Small forms

### Ch. 6 · Presenting
- Grouping phrases into units lets the listener learn the material. Beginnings stable, surprises later. Shortening phrases presses forward; lengthening relaxes.
- **App:** *The sentence and liquidation*; *Exploring*.

### Ch. 7 · One-part forms
- One idea, no strong internal punctuation, so the piece must build a progression to a climax near the end. Rachmaninoff's rising waves; changing the figuration at the end as a gesture of finality.
- **App:** *Riff cycles as variations*.

### Ch. 8 · Ternary form
- A B A with contrast in motive, register, articulation, texture; the return often has a more restless accompaniment. Abrupt transitions work in inverse proportion to their frequency.
- **App:** *Contrast and transition*; *Returning and ending*.

### Ch. 9 · Binary form (skimmed)
- Both halves on the same idea; the second half is less stable and raises the temperature. First ending less final than the second.

### Ch. 10 · Variation form
- Simple memorable theme; one character per variation; **group the variations** into larger progressions; **move the climax**; mode change for instant colour; **the last variation breaks the pattern**; continuous variations for short themes.
- **App:** Module 4 — *Riff cycles as variations*.

## Part 3 — Principles of larger forms

### Ch. 11 · Contrasting
- **Contrast scale 1–10**: how many dimensions, how much, how fast. Register and timbre outweigh harmony. Longer pieces need bigger contrasts but more continuity overall; repeated contrasts weaken.
- **App:** *Contrast and transition*.

### Ch. 12 · Connecting
- Transition procedure: list every difference, change one at a time, length follows from the number of changes. Other routes: turning point, interruption, cross-cutting. Transitions as whole sections (overlap, counterpoint, step-by-step mutation).
- **App:** *Contrast and transition*.

### Ch. 13 · Progressing
- Direction binds a large work. **Climax hierarchy**: equal climaxes seem to weaken. Rising outweighs falling; keep falls short. Simplify and accelerate before the final climax; hold something back.
- **App:** *Building tension*; *Where the climax goes*; the plan checker.

### Ch. 14 · Rondo (skimmed)
- Refrain and episodes, squareness softened by transitions. Haydn: episodes start with the refrain's motive, false reprises, varied refrains over the same skeleton.
- **App:** *Returning and ending*.

### Ch. 15 · Beginning
- Grab attention, state a character, leave a question open. Provocation: one dimension contradicts the others. Seven opening types. What doesn't work as a beginning. Introductions: slower, audibly incomplete, can return as an anomaly.
- **App:** Module 4 — *Beginnings and introductions*.

### Ch. 16 · Exploring
- Development = known material in new contexts. Unstable phrase structure, counterpoint and overlap, remote variants. Interruption (prepared; repeated interruption becomes dialogue; a big one is a gear change). Avoid "alphabet form". **Formal anomalies** planted early and paid off late.
- **App:** *Exploring: instability, interruption, anomaly*.

### Ch. 17 · Returning
- Music must breathe through familiarity. Return types from literal to varied to one-critical-change. A return always has a changed context. Build an expectation, then thwart it once.
- **App:** *Returning and ending*.

### Ch. 18 · Ending
- Memory keeps beginning, major climaxes and contrasts, ending. Too much strong mid-piece punctuation is the common fault. Climactic, fade-out and rounding-off endings; **every dimension agrees at the end**; the coda as an extended, repeatedly avoided arrival.
- **App:** *Returning and ending*; *Where the climax goes*; the plan checker.

### Ch. 19 · Sonata form (skimmed)
- Major contrast, long-unresolved issues, a large return for balance. The recapitulation has one critical change.

### Ch. 20 · Refinements
- **Announcing**: build tension that resolves exactly on the important event, with preparation proportional to its importance. **Accenting**: momentary novelty, coordinated across two or more dimensions, in four grades. **Enriching**: background planes of movement and resonance, kept from competing by timbre, register, speed and rhythm.
- **App:** *Exploring* (announcing); Module 3 (planned *Layers and the sound-box*).

### Conclusion · From craft to art
- **The coordination principle**: no single dimension convinces alone; full alignment only at major climaxes and endings. Principles, not moulds. Criteria: force, balance, range.
- **App:** *Designing the whole*.

### Appendix A · Sketching
- One idea per card; explore variants; plan each idea's career; extend; shuffle orders; decide each join; rough draft; rest; listen as an outsider; then details.
- **App:** *Designing the whole*; Module 0's riff board.

## The book in five rules

1. Rate it: similarity 1–5, punctuation 1–10, contrast 1–10. Numbers make form discussable in a band.
2. Count the dimensions: punctuation, contrast and climax strength all come from how many dimensions move together.
3. Budget surprise: big contrasts, strong stops and full alignment are spent, not free.
4. Hierarchy: each peak higher, each seam weaker than the ending, the last moment the biggest.
5. Familiar and new at once: in every transition, every return, every development.
