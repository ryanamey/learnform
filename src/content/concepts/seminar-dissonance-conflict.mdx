## The question

When a guitar riff groups in threes against drums in fours, is the listener hearing **two meters in conflict**, or **one meter** with a rhythm set against it? The answer changes how you write and rehearse: whether you treat the second layer as a meter of its own, or as tension against the one meter the band holds.

## Krebs and Cohn: two layers in conflict

For Krebs, meter is the union of all regular layers, and grouping and displacement dissonance are conflicts between layers <Source book="krebs" where="Ch. 2" />. One layer, the **primary metrical consonance**, usually the notated meter, stays present as a reference even when only the conflicting layer sounds (subliminal dissonance). The analogy is with pitch: a dissonance is heard against a background triad.

Cohn formalises the layers and shows them at several levels at once <Source book="cohn" where="§II–IV" />. But he raises a doubt himself. When a six-bar span can be heard either in twos or in threes, it isn't clear that a listener can hold both at once. It may be a "rabbit/duck" figure, seen one way or the other but never both. If so, he says, the word "dissonance" may not fit, just as a pivot chord isn't dissonant for belonging to two keys. He also rejects the idea that a primary consonance must govern a whole piece: the K. 550 minuet ends without settling whether its hypermeter is duple or triple.

## London: one meter at a time

London argues from experiments that listeners hold one meter at a time <Source book="london" where="Ch. 3, 5" />. In a polyrhythm they either fuse the parts into one pattern or follow one and hear the other against it. "Metric dissonance" is acceptable as a metaphor for the tension, but it is never two meters.

## Hasty: no conflict at all

Hasty goes further <Source book="hasty" where="Ch. 11, 14" />. Meter is projection, so there is no fixed meter for a rhythm to conflict with. There are only projections that are confirmed, denied or reinterpreted, and these include the "phenomenal" accents others call rhythm. What gets called metrical conflict he describes as competing projective potentials, and ambiguity is a positive state, not vagueness.

## Yust: several rhythms, one meter

Yust offers a way between them <Source book="yust" where="Ch. 8.2" />. Distinguish **rhythmic structure** from **metrical structure**. A passage can have several rhythmic structures: one for each part, and one for the whole texture. It has only one meter, the listener's. On his account Krebs's "antimetrical layers" are rhythmic structures, so his analyses survive London's objection. The rhythm of the whole texture is what decides the meter that is felt. He also reads one famous case (Beethoven's "Ghost" Trio) differently from Krebs: as a mixture of meters within one hierarchy, not one meter with a hidden layer under it.

## Hear the question

Play the hemiola in *Grouping and displacement* and the 7-against-4 in *Polyrhythm*. Can you hear both layers at once, or do you switch between them? Does it change if you tap the drums or the guitar?

## Where they agree

All five agree that something is felt as tension when layers don't line up, and that one layer usually acts as the reference. All five agree that performance (what is loud and steady) affects which layer that is.

## Where they differ

- **How many meters?** Two or more layers count as meter (Krebs, and Cohn with doubts); one meter and other rhythms (London, Yust); no fixed meter at all (Hasty).
- **Must dissonance resolve?** Krebs's narratives often move toward resolution; Cohn warns against assuming it must.
- **Is ambiguity a state of conflict or of richness?** Krebs: tension. Hasty: a positive, definite experience of two possibilities.

## Questions to decide

1. When the band plays one layer against another, which one do you want the audience to hold as the meter?
2. Will you notate both layers with their own time signatures (as Magadini suggests), or keep one meter and mark the other as accents?
3. In your songs, should a conflict always come back to agreement, or can a section end unresolved?
