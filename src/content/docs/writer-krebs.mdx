## Harald Krebs, *Fantasy Pieces*

Krebs is a music theorist at the University of Victoria. *Fantasy Pieces: Metrical Dissonance in the Music of Robert Schumann* was published by Oxford in 1999. The book alternates theory chapters with fictional "intermezzi" in which Schumann's alter egos, Florestan and Eusebius, discuss the ideas.

**How it was read for the app:** Ch. 2, 4, 6, 7, the glossary and the epilogue in full; Ch. 1 now read (the fictional opening in full, the twentieth-century survey in large part); Ch. 5 at its opening; Ch. 3 and 8 skimmed.

## His question

**How do conflicts between layers of motion work as dissonances, and what do they do over a whole piece?**

## His toolkit

- **Layers**: pulse layer, interpretive layers, the metrical layer and antimetrical layers (Ch. 2).
- **Two kinds of metrical dissonance**: **grouping** (G, noncongruent layers such as 3 against 2) and **displacement** (D, congruent layers shifted) (Ch. 2).
- **Direct vs indirect** (at once, or one after the other); **subliminal** (only the antimetrical layer sounds) (Ch. 2).
- **Intensity**: inherent and contextual (Ch. 2).
- **Metrical progressions and processes**: consonance–dissonance–consonance narratives, intensification, maps (Ch. 4).
- **Ties to form, pitch and text** (Ch. 6); **performing** dissonances (Ch. 7).
- **History** (Ch. 1): two kinds of conflict already in Fétis, Berlioz and Riemann; Fétis's "mutation", where an ambiguity prepares a change of meter.
- **Composers' revisions** (Ch. 5): Schumann added dissonance to passages first written consonant, by inserting segments, adding a dissonant layer, or shifting an existing one.

## Where he agrees with the others

- **Cohn** formalises his ideas (pure and mixed spans, double hemiola). **Biamonte** applies them to rock.
- With **Yust**: displacement as a transformation of a regular rhythm.

## Where he differs

- **Two meters at once.** London (as Yust reports him) and Hasty reject conflicting meters. Yust reframes Krebs's layers as rhythmic, not metrical, structures. See *Seminar: Is metric dissonance a conflict?*
- **A primary metrical consonance as a global reference.** Cohn shows a Mozart minuet that ends without settling which one it is.
- **Beethoven's "Ghost" Trio**: two layers with one submerged (Krebs) vs a mixture of meters in one hierarchy (Yust).
