## The question

Is a bar of 7 one long cycle, or a group of 2s and 3s? How long can a cycle be before nobody feels it? The writers give different reasons for where the limits lie.

## London: uneven beats on an even pulse

London's **non-isochronous meters** have a steady fast pulse and uneven beats of 2s and 3s <Source book="london" where="Ch. 7–8" />. His rule of **maximal evenness**: beats are spread as evenly as the number of pulses allows (9 as 2-2-2-3 or 3-3-3, not 2-3-4). Prime lengths force unevenness. His time limits bound the whole thing: beats between about 200 ms and 2 seconds, a preference around 600 ms, and a cycle that can't be much longer than about five or six seconds and still be heard as one measure.

## Snyder: compounds and schemas

Snyder hears groups longer than about four beats as compound: 5 as 3+2 or 2+3, larger meters pulling apart into smaller ones <Source book="snyder" where="Ch. 12" />. Cycles longer than short-term memory become schemas, learned rather than heard whole; very long meters use strict repetition and the same drum sound on the same beat each cycle.

## Prieto: the numbers are the material

Prieto teaches rhythm as strings of grouping numbers; the string is varied by reordering, and cycles of different lengths are combined and realigned <Source book="prieto" where="Levels 1–3" />. Sriram's Karnatic sources describe very long cycles (talas) tracked by hand gestures <Source book="sriram" where="Taalam" />.

## Biamonte and Pieslak: stable or unstable?

Biamonte, following London, treats a steady odd meter as consonant but less stable than 4/4; what can't be entrained is a meter that keeps changing with no larger period <Source book="biamonte" where="§1–2" />. Pieslak describes Meshuggah fitting odd riffs to a symmetrical frame, so the long cycle underneath stays square <Source book="pieslak" where="pp. 222–225" />.

## Schoenberg and Caplin

Neither writes about additive meters. Schoenberg's irregular lengths are counted in bars, with three causes (the length of units, their number, a mix) and the demand that they have a reason <Source book="schoenberg" where="Ch. XIV" />. Caplin's deviations (extension, expansion, compression) explain odd bar counts as stretched or shortened functions <Source book="caplin" where="Ch. 5" />.

## Hear the question

Play the 11-beat cycle with and without landmarks (*Meter as a schema*). Then write 2-2-2-3 and 2-3-4 (the Guitar Pro exercise in *Odd meters*) and loop them. Which ones can you clap after two hearings?

## Where they agree

Odd groupings are built from 2s and 3s. Long cycles need something steady to be followed. There is an upper limit somewhere around a few seconds for a cycle heard whole.

## Where they differ

- **What makes an odd meter hearable:** maximal evenness (London), landmark sounds and repetition (Snyder), a counted frame (Pieslak), or the number string itself (Prieto).
- **Is a steady odd meter tension?** Biamonte: not by itself. Pieslak's Meshuggah: the tension comes from the riff against a square frame.
- **Odd bar counts:** additive meter (London, Prieto) versus stretched functions (Caplin) versus justified irregularity (Schoenberg).
