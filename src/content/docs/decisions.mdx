### 2026-11-10 · Psychology is a layer across all modules, plus Module 5 "The listener"

**Decision:** Listener psychology isn't a separate subject. Every concept page gets an evidence-graded "Why it works on the listener" section. A new Module 5 brings it together, and its tool is a listening-test kit. The earlier Modules 5 and 6 become 6 and 7.
**Why:** Form exists in the listener's memory, expectation and body. The band concluded that psychology matters as much as form, and the research supports treating the two as one design problem.
**Evidence rule:** Every principle carries a grade (strong, moderate, weak, speculative). Hypnosis techniques from Erickson and Elman are used only as labeled craft analogies, because no research links them to composition. Wier is not cited.
**Would change if:** our own listening tests contradict a principle, in which case our data wins for our music.

### 2026-11-10 · Treat large-scale proportion as private scaffolding

**Decision:** Fibonacci and golden-section lengths and key plans may generate material, but the form has to be audible through motif, timbre and density.
**Why:** Cook (1987) and Lalitte & Bigand (2006) found listeners barely hear large-scale tonal closure and only partly notice section order.

### 2026-11-10 · The source list is built from a verified search, not a suggested reading list

**Decision:** The original reading list came from a ChatGPT-suggested curriculum. It is replaced by a master source list built from a structured search in six areas: Tool scholarship, prog and metal analysis, rock form theory, rhythm and meter, texture/voice/album, and craft/process/tools. Every source was checked against a publisher, journal or repository page. Anything that couldn't be checked is marked unverified.
**Why:** The roadmap is the blueprint for everything else, and nobody had checked it against what the books actually contain.
**Would change if:** a full read of a source contradicts its card. Each full read updates the coverage matrix.

### 2026-11-10 · Read books in full, one at a time, starting with Belkin

**Decision:** After the source survey, read sources completely, in priority order. Each finished book updates the concept pages and gets a chapter guide.
**Why:** Module 1 was built from selected chapters and cited some chapters that weren't read (Snyder ch. 4, Caplin). Concept pages now show how much of their source material has actually been read.

### 2026-11-10 · Pages are organized by concept, not by book

**Decision:** Learning pages are concepts that draw on several sources. Books get chapter guides and source cards, not their own sections.
**Why:** Several books cover the same ideas. Organizing by book would teach the same concept four times in four vocabularies.

### 2026-11-10 · Keep self-hosted Better Auth instead of Neon's managed auth

**Decision:** Better Auth runs inside the app, with its tables in our Neon database through Drizzle.
**Why:** It was already built and tested. Switching would mean a second login system and moving the app's tables to Neon's user IDs.
**Would change if:** the band needs features like social login or organizations that the managed service makes easier.

### 2026-11-10 · Dev and production are separate Neon branches

**Decision:** The `dev` branch is used locally and `production` for Vercel. Accounts don't carry over between them.

### 2026-11-10 · Compose every week from week 1

**Decision:** Every concept has an exercise and an EP task. The EP runs alongside the study.
**Why:** In the original curriculum composing came last, in month 12. Writing is how a personal voice develops; theory is for diagnosing what you wrote.

### 2026-11-10 · Three analysis maps, not ten

**Decision:** Every analysis uses a material map, an energy curve and a meter map. Other maps are added only when a piece needs them.
**Why:** Ten maps per piece leads to burnout, and the three cover the essentials.
