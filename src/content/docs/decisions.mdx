### 2026-11-10 · *Analyzing Popular Music* read in full

**Decision:** The essay collection Ali supplied (Moore, ed., 2003; not *Song Means*) is read in full and logged as a selective source. What it teaches about form went into existing pages: Bowman's song-length additive build in Otis Redding's "Try a Little Tenderness" (*Where the climax goes*), long-form repetition in house and drum'n'bass (*Repetition, process and absorption*), Jethro Tull's displacement and gesture exchange (*Riff cycles as variations*), same words in two settings and Covach's formal parenthesis (*Contrast and transition*), a change carried into later returns (*Returning and ending*), surface vs context fragmentation (*Moment form and strata*), musical worlding and listening situations (*What listeners actually hear*).
**Backlog:** Nine new Roadmap entries, mostly for Module 6 (verbal space, lyric vs anti-lyric, vocal performance) and Module 3's sound-box page (detuned layers, timbre as marker).
**Why selective:** Half the book is about method and cultural theory (Walser, Krims, Kennett, Stokes); it's summarised in the guide but doesn't change how to build a form.

### 2026-11-10 · Kramer read in full; time becomes its own thread in Module 4

**Decision:** Four new Module 4 pages: *Kinds of musical time* (goal-directed, nondirected, multiply-directed, moment, vertical), *Gestural time* (a passage's function vs. its position: ending gestures at the start, false endings), *Moment form* (start/stop vs. begin/end, Cone's stratification, interlock and synthesis), and *Time, information and proportion* (why remembered length depends on information, not the clock). One new Module 2 page: *Accent and hypermeter* (phenomenal, structural and metric accent; extension, contraction, overlap). Five new labs. The form-plan check gained a "stop" ending for moment-form designs.
**Also:** *Trance and the listening test* gets Kramer's vertical time (boredom vs. timelessness); *Repetition and process* gets the anatomy of a process piece; *What listeners hear* gets his caution on proportions.
**Caution kept:** Kramer's proportion claims (golden-section timing, durations heard as ratios) are hypotheses with mixed evidence. The app teaches them as a way to plan and check, not as a law of perception.

### 2026-11-10 · Snyder's form chapter becomes a Module 4 page

**Decision:** New page *How a form is remembered* (Module 4, before *Designing the whole*), with a lab that tests Snyder's rule of thumb: one changed parameter is an articulation, three or more is a new section, and a change that doesn't last is an interruption. The plan checker now flags sections under 10 seconds.
**Also:** Module 1's memory figures corrected against the full ch. 4 (3–5 s average, up to 10–12 s with simple material; 7 ± 2 with rehearsal, 3–4 without; focus of awareness about 3). Parallelism (ch. 3) now backs the "further apart, closer variant" rule.

### 2026-11-10 · Psychology is a layer across all modules, plus Module 5 "The listener"

**Decision:** Listener psychology isn't a separate subject. Every concept page gets an evidence-graded "Why it works on the listener" section. A new Module 5 brings it together, and its tool is a listening-test kit. The earlier Modules 5 and 6 become 6 and 7.
**Why:** Form exists in the listener's memory, expectation and body. The band concluded that psychology matters as much as form, and the research supports treating the two as one design problem.
**Evidence rule:** Every principle carries a grade (strong, moderate, weak, speculative). Hypnosis techniques from Erickson and Elman are used only as labeled craft analogies, because no research links them to composition. Wier is not cited.
**Would change if:** our own listening tests contradict a principle, in which case our data wins for our music.

### 2026-11-10 · Treat large-scale proportion as private scaffolding

**Decision:** Fibonacci and golden-section lengths and key plans may generate material, but the form has to be audible through motif, timbre and density.
**Why:** Cook (1987) and Lalitte & Bigand (2006) found listeners barely hear large-scale tonal closure and only partly notice section order.

### 2026-11-10 · The source list is built from a verified search, not a suggested reading list

**Decision:** The original reading list came from a ChatGPT-suggested curriculum. It is replaced by a master source list built from a structured search in six areas: Tool scholarship, prog and metal analysis, rock form theory, rhythm and meter, texture/voice/album, and craft/process/tools. ### 2026-11-10 · Three analysis maps, not ten

**Decision:** Every analysis uses a material map, an energy curve and a meter map. Other maps are added only when a piece needs them.
**Why:** Ten maps per piece leads to burnout, and the three cover the essentials.
