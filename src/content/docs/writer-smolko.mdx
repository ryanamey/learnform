## Tim Smolko, *Thick as a Brick*

Smolko wrote "Jethro Tull's *Thick as a Brick*: Geared toward the Exceptional rather than the Average" as an MA thesis at the University of Georgia (2011). It is a detailed study of a 43-minute album built as one continuous track: how a progressive rock band holds a piece that long together.

**How it was read for the app:** ch. 1's sections on the long song; the lai section of ch. 2; ch. 5's sections on form, song forms, repetition and Motives 1–7, instrumental passages, style, meter, texture and dynamics; the conclusions. The instrumentation table and band biographies were skimmed. Not read: the packaging, lyrics, harmony and live-version sections.

## His question

**How does a single 43-minute track hold together, and what techniques make it more than a medley of songs?**

## His toolkit

- **Closed song forms alternating with open, through-composed passages** (ch. 5).
- **Three kinds of repetition**: local, reprise, thematic development (ch. 5).
- **Motives as leitmotifs**: returning on new instruments, in new registers and new meters; **layered at the climax** (ch. 5).
- **Four functions of instrumental passages**: transition, soloing, contrast, repetition (ch. 5).
- **Tapestry vs quilt**: styles blended vs seams showing (ch. 5).
- **Four kinds of textural contrast**: homorhythmic vs polyrhythmic, one instrument vs the group, gradual addition and subtraction, sound vs silence (ch. 5).
- **Dynamics**: abrupt contrasts, terraced dynamics, the long gradual crescendo (ch. 5).

## Where he agrees with the others

- With **LaRue**: contrasts of texture and dynamics articulate a long form; motives return in new colours.
- With **Covach**: progressive rock uses texture to give long pieces large contrasts.
- With **Caplin**: transitions destabilise and loosen, with fragmentation and sequence.

## Where he differs

- He stresses that in progressive rock the bass and drums are often **melodic and competing**, not only accompanying. This sits against the layer model (Moore), where each layer has one job.
- He finds long gradual crescendos common in prog and rare in mainstream rock, a stylistic claim based on his examples rather than a survey.

## Where the app uses him

Course IV: *Textural rhythm*, *Dynamics, builds and terraces*, *Texture as narrative*. Course XIII: the album as form.

## How to read him

Ch. 5 is the core. Read the form and repetition sections first, then the sections on instrumental passages, texture and dynamics.
