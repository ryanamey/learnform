## Designing form before the material exists

Your band already works the way this module needs. You and the drummer design forms, often remotely, and the others create material to fit. That only works with a **shared, precise language** for sections. Tool solved the same problem with a whiteboard of riff names and beat counts, because each member counted differently <Source book="tool-interviews" />.

A riff board is that whiteboard, made explicit. A **form plan** is the arrangement of riff-board entries, plus the instructions the material-makers need.

<KeyIdea>Name it, count it, give it a job. A riff without a name and a count can't be designed with. A section without a job can't be judged.</KeyIdea>

## The riff board: one card per unit

<Steps title="Every card has six fields">

1. **Name:** short and memorable ("Spiral", "Gate", "Low Tide"). Names stick in memory better than numbers or letters (Snyder: labelled chunks are easier to hold and recall).
2. **Count:** total length and its grouping, for example `7/8 = 2+2+3`, or `25 sixteenths in a 4-bar frame`.
3. **Material:** the motif in notation (Motif bank) or a Guitar Pro / MIDI file.
4. **Family:** which motif it comes from, and its distance on the 1–5 scale (Module 1).
5. **Feel:** half, normal, double or quarter time, and the cymbal layer (Module 2).
6. **Job:** what it does in a form. For example: *present*, *repeat / establish*, *destabilize*, *build*, *cue*, *arrive*, *release*, *carry* (a background ostinato), *break*.

</Steps>

The motif bank already holds names, notation, family and distance. Put the count, feel and job in its Notes field until the dedicated riff-board tool arrives.

## The form plan: what you hand to the band

A form plan is a table. Each row is one section of the song:

| # | Section | Riff(s) | Bars × count | Feel | Energy (0–10) | Job | Notes for the band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intro | Low Tide | 8 × 4/4 | half | 2 | present | clean, guitar synth pad only |
| 2 | V1 | Spiral | 4 × (7+7+7+5) | normal | 4 | establish | vocals enter bar 3 |
| 3 | Build | Spiral → fragment | 6 × 4/4 | normal → double | 4 → 8 | build | cut riff to first 3 notes, rising |
| 4 | Gate | Gate | 1 bar | — | 0 | cue (withholding) | full stop, single hi-hat |
| 5 | Climax | Gate (new) | 8 × 6/8 | half | 10 | arrive | only time this appears |

The *Energy* and *Job* columns are where Modules 3 and 5 come in: they turn the table from a list of parts into a designed shape.

## In Guitar Pro

- Put a **section marker** at every row of the plan, using the same names as the riff board.
- Keep a **"Form" track** (any instrument, muted) where each bar carries a text annotation of the section's job and energy number.
- When you send a plan to the band, the file should hold **structure and counts first**: drums and a guide riff. Material comes back from them as new tracks.

<ListenerSide grade="moderate">

Snyder: short-term memory holds a few seconds and a handful of chunks. Anything longer is reconstructed from long-term memory, which keeps a sketch rather than detail. Named, counted units with clear jobs are how *you* keep a 10-minute form in your head. They also predict what a listener will be able to hold onto.

</ListenerSide>

<Exercise title="Board your own material">

1. Take 8–12 riffs you already have. Make a card for each with all six fields.
2. Sort the cards by **job**. Which jobs have no candidates? (Usually *carry*, *cue* and *release*.) That gap is what your next writing session should produce.
3. Arrange six to eight cards into a form plan with an energy column. Draw the energy numbers as a line. Is the peak where you want it?

</Exercise>
